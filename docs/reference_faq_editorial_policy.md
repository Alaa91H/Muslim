# FAQ and Common Misconceptions Editorial Policy

This document governs the `reference_faq_misconceptions_v2.json` book.

## Purpose

The FAQ layer is a short-answer entry point into the larger Islamic Reference Library. It should resolve common confusion quickly, then route the reader to the longer sourced article. It is not a replacement for a personalized fatwa, judicial ruling, medical advice, or specialist historical review.

## Answer structure

Every FAQ must contain:

- one concise direct answer in Arabic and English;
- one context/nuance paragraph in both languages;
- at least one structured citation;
- at least one related long-form library topic;
- an explicit editorial state.

Answers should distinguish:
- primary revelation from commentary;
- established doctrine from juristic disagreement;
- religious rule from local culture;
- sacred status from historical importance;
- historical description from modern political judgment;
- general education from case-specific fatwa.

## High-sensitivity questions

The following areas require specialist review before an article can move from `NeedsReview` to `Reviewed`:

- takfir and sectarian identity;
- jihad, armed conflict, and treatment of noncombatants;
- family law, marriage consent, divorce, inheritance, and testimony;
- claims about women that generalize from one legal rule to spiritual worth;
- contested historical narratives;
- Quranic abrogation, qira'at, and hadith-authenticity claims;
- any answer that could be mistaken for a legal instruction to act against another person.

The FAQ must never turn a historical grievance, religious slogan, or isolated quotation into authorization for violence or vigilantism.

## Misconception handling

Question titles may repeat a misconception because users search for that wording. The answer itself must not sensationalize the claim. Prefer:
1. a direct correction;
2. the minimum context necessary to understand why the misconception arises;
3. a link to the detailed article.

Avoid treating all critics as hostile or all Muslim historical practice as identical to Islamic doctrine.

## Review status

All entries in the first edition remain `NeedsReview`. A future reviewer should verify:
- citations and locators;
- Arabic and English semantic parity;
- whether the short answer overstates consensus;
- whether legal-school differences need to be named;
- whether a historical claim needs an external academic source;
- whether the answer stays useful when read outside its chapter.
